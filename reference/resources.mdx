This page brings together the most important resources available to you as an employee — from foundational documents and everyday productivity tools to learning opportunities and support contacts. Bookmark this page and return to it whenever you need a quick starting point, especially during your first weeks in a new role.

## Essential Reading

Every employee benefits from familiarity with these core documents. Locate each one through your HR portal or internal intranet, and review them early in your onboarding process.

- **Employee Handbook** — Your primary reference for company policies, benefits, code of conduct, leave rules, and compliance requirements. Read this first to understand the guardrails and expectations that apply to everyone in the organization.

- **Role Description** — A detailed breakdown of your specific position: core responsibilities, success criteria, required skills, and how your role fits within the team. Use it to align with your manager on expectations from day one.

- **Org Chart** — A visual map of the organization's structure, showing reporting relationships, team groupings, and leadership hierarchy. The org chart helps you understand who to contact for different needs and how decisions flow.

- **Team Norms Document** — A shared agreement that defines how your immediate team works together — meeting cadences, communication preferences, response time expectations, and decision-making processes. If your team doesn't have one, the [Best Practices](/core/best-practices) page explains how to create one.

- **Style Guide** — Guidelines for written and visual communication standards, including tone of voice, formatting conventions, and brand usage. Essential for anyone producing documentation, external communications, or presentations.

## Learning & Development

Investing in your growth is part of doing your job well. These resource types are available to support your development at every stage of your career.

- **Internal Training Programs** — Your organization likely offers structured training for new hires (role-specific onboarding), skills development (writing, presenting, data analysis), and systems training (using tools and platforms). Check your learning management system or HR portal for a current catalog of available courses and required completions.

- **External Courses and Certifications** — Many organizations provide a learning stipend or reimburse costs for relevant external courses, certifications, and online learning platforms. Before enrolling, confirm your organization's reimbursement policy and pre-approval requirements with HR. Prioritize credentials that align with your current role or a defined career path.

- **Mentorship Programs** — A mentorship relationship pairs you with a more experienced colleague who offers guidance, feedback, and career perspective outside your direct reporting line. Ask your manager or HR team whether a formal mentorship program exists, or take the initiative to propose an informal arrangement with someone whose career trajectory you admire.

- **Conferences and Industry Events** — Attending conferences — whether in person or virtually — exposes you to new ideas, industry trends, and professional networks beyond your organization. Bring a clear learning objective to each event and share key takeaways with your team afterward to multiply the value of your attendance.

## Getting Help

Knowing who to contact saves time and reduces frustration. Reach out to the right person for the right type of issue.

**IT Support** — For hardware issues, software access requests, account lockouts, network problems, or security concerns, contact your IT helpdesk. Most organizations offer a ticketing portal, live chat, and a phone line for urgent issues. Include clear details about the problem, your device, and any error messages you've seen to speed up resolution.

**HR Questions** — For questions about payroll, benefits, leave policies, performance processes, workplace conduct, or career progression, contact your HR business partner or the general HR team inbox. For sensitive matters, HR representatives are trained to handle inquiries with confidentiality.

**Knowledge Base Updates** — To report errors, suggest new content, or ask about the status of a pending article request, contact the KB administrator through the feedback link on any article page or via the internal support channel designated for KB requests. Response times are typically within three business days.
