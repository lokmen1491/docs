This glossary covers the most common workplace and job-related terminology you'll encounter while navigating the Job Knowledge Base and your day-to-day work. Whether you're new to a role or looking to align on shared language with your team, these definitions give you a clear, consistent foundation. Terms are organized alphabetically for quick reference.

## A – C

**Accountability** — The obligation to accept responsibility for your actions, decisions, and outcomes. Being accountable means you own your work, follow through on commitments, and report honestly on progress.

**Asynchronous Communication** — A communication style where participants don't need to be present at the same time. Examples include email, recorded video updates, and shared documents. Async communication supports distributed and remote teams by reducing the need for real-time meetings.

**Bandwidth** — A colloquial term for a person's available capacity to take on work. Saying you have "low bandwidth" signals that your schedule is full and you cannot absorb additional tasks without trade-offs.

**Blockers** — Any obstacle, dependency, or issue preventing progress on a task or project. Identifying blockers early allows teams to escalate or re-route work before deadlines are missed.

**Capacity Planning** — The process of determining how much work a team or individual can realistically handle within a given timeframe, balancing workload against available resources and skills.

## D – F

**Deliverable** — A specific, tangible output or result that must be produced and handed off as part of a project or task. Deliverables have clear criteria for completion and are typically tied to milestones or deadlines.

**Escalation** — The process of raising an issue, decision, or risk to a higher authority or level of responsibility when it cannot be resolved at the current level. Timely escalation prevents small problems from becoming larger ones.

**Feedback Loop** — A cycle in which the output of a process or action is reviewed and the findings are used to inform or improve the next iteration. Regular feedback loops promote continuous improvement and alignment.

## G – M

**Goal Setting** — The practice of defining specific, measurable outcomes you intend to achieve within a set timeframe. Effective goal setting typically follows frameworks like SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound).

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)** — A measurable value that demonstrates how effectively an individual, team, or organization is achieving a key objective. KPIs are used to evaluate success and guide decision-making over time.

**Milestone** — A significant checkpoint or event in a project that marks the completion of a major phase or deliverable. Milestones help teams track progress and confirm that a project is on schedule.

## N – R

**OKR (Objectives and Key Results)** — A goal-setting framework that pairs a qualitative objective (what you want to achieve) with 2–5 measurable key results (how you'll know you've achieved it). OKRs are commonly set quarterly at the individual, team, and organizational level.

**Onboarding** — The structured process of integrating a new employee into an organization. Effective onboarding covers role expectations, tools access, team introductions, and cultural orientation to accelerate time-to-productivity.

**Performance Review** — A formal evaluation process in which an employee's work, accomplishments, and areas for development are assessed — typically by a manager — over a defined review period. Reviews often inform compensation, promotions, and development plans.

**Priority Matrix** — A decision-making tool that categorizes tasks by urgency and importance (often called the Eisenhower Matrix). It helps you focus on high-impact work and intentionally deprioritize or delegate lower-value tasks.

**Retrospective** — A structured meeting held at the end of a project, sprint, or work cycle in which a team reflects on what went well, what didn't, and what to do differently next time. Retrospectives are a core practice in agile workflows.

## S – Z

**Scope Creep** — The gradual, uncontrolled expansion of a project's scope beyond its original boundaries — typically without corresponding adjustments to timeline, budget, or resources. Managing scope creep requires clear documentation and disciplined change-control processes.

**SLA (Service Level Agreement)** — A formal commitment between a service provider and a recipient that defines the expected level of service, including response times, quality standards, and resolution timelines.

**Sprint** — A short, fixed-length work cycle — typically one to four weeks — used in agile project management. Each sprint ends with a reviewable set of completed work and feeds into a retrospective and planning session for the next sprint.

**Stakeholder** — Any individual, team, or organization with an interest in or influence over a project's outcome. Stakeholders may include customers, leadership, team members, or external partners who should be kept informed and engaged throughout a project.

**Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** — A hierarchical decomposition of a project into smaller, manageable components or tasks. A WBS makes complex projects easier to plan, assign, and track by breaking large efforts into discrete work packages.
