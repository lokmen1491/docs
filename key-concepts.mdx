Before you work through guides, workflows, or reference material, it helps to understand the core concepts that shape how this knowledge base is built and how its content is meant to be used. This page defines the key terms and mental models you'll encounter throughout the Job Knowledge Base. Each definition goes beyond a simple dictionary entry — it explains the concept's purpose, how it applies to your daily work, and how it connects to other areas of the knowledge base. Read through these once when you start, and return to this page anytime a term needs clarification.

## Core Concepts

<Accordion title="Knowledge Article">
  A **Knowledge Article** is any discrete, self-contained page within the Job Knowledge Base that addresses a specific topic, task, or question. Every article is written with a clear goal in mind — to give you exactly the information you need without requiring you to read surrounding context first.

  Knowledge articles come in several forms: explanatory pages that describe how something works, step-by-step guides that walk you through a task, and reference pages you consult for quick lookups. What makes something a knowledge article is that it stands on its own — you can arrive at it directly from search and leave with your question answered.

  When you contribute to the knowledge base (or request new content), think in terms of articles: what single, specific topic does this page address, and what does someone need to know or do after reading it?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Workflow">
  A **Workflow** is a documented sequence of steps that accomplishes a defined work outcome. Workflows in this knowledge base are written to be repeatable — they're designed so that any person in the relevant role can follow them and achieve the same result, regardless of prior experience.

  Workflows differ from casual instructions in that they are structured, tested, and maintained. They account for the most common variations and edge cases, and they specify not just *what* to do but *why* each step matters. You'll find workflows documented in the [Core](/core/workflows) section and embedded within guides throughout the knowledge base.

  When you're executing a task you've done before, a workflow helps you stay consistent and avoid errors. When you're doing something new, a workflow gives you a reliable foundation to build on rather than starting from scratch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Best Practice">
  A **Best Practice** is a method or approach that experience and evidence have shown to produce better outcomes than alternatives. Best practices in this knowledge base are not rigid rules — they're well-reasoned recommendations that reflect how skilled professionals approach common challenges.

  Best practices are distinct from workflows in that they describe *how* to approach something rather than the exact steps to take. For example, a workflow might specify how to complete a weekly report, while a best practice might recommend always reviewing the previous week's data before writing the summary.

  You'll find best practices called out throughout the knowledge base using `<Tip>` and `<Note>` callouts, and collected together in the [Best Practices](/core/best-practices) reference page. When you see a best practice noted, treat it as guidance from someone who has already learned why the alternative approach causes problems.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Quick Reference">
  A **Quick Reference** is a page or section designed for scanning rather than reading. Quick references present information in the most compact, accessible format possible — typically using tables, short definitions, bullet points, or code examples — so you can find a specific fact in seconds.

  The [Glossary](/reference/glossary) is the primary quick reference in this knowledge base. The [FAQ](/reference/faq) also functions as a quick reference for common questions. You should not read quick reference pages from top to bottom; instead, search or scan them for the specific item you need.

  When you encounter an unfamiliar term mid-task and don't want to lose your place, a quick reference page is where to go. Bookmark your most-used quick references so you can reach them without navigating through the full site structure.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Guide">
  A **Guide** is an end-to-end walkthrough that takes you from a starting state to a completed outcome. Guides in this knowledge base are written for a specific audience and scenario — they don't try to cover every possible case, but instead give you a clear, tested path through the most common version of a task.

  Guides are longer and more narrative than quick references. They include context, explain decisions, and often include tips and warnings that help you avoid common mistakes. The [Guides](/guides/getting-set-up) section contains guides organized by complexity: start with *Getting Set Up* for foundational tasks, move to *Common Tasks* for day-to-day work, and consult *Advanced Usage* when you're ready for more complex scenarios.

  If you're ever unsure how to complete a task, look for a guide first. If no guide exists for your scenario, the [FAQ](/reference/faq) and [Resources](/reference/resources) pages may point you to additional help.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Resource">
  A **Resource** is any external tool, document, template, or link that supports your work but lives outside the core content of the knowledge base. Resources are collected and curated in the [Resources](/reference/resources) reference page so you can find them without searching the wider internet.

  Resources might include downloadable templates, links to official documentation for tools your organization uses, training materials, or connections to other internal systems. They're maintained alongside the knowledge base content to ensure the links stay current and relevant.

  When you complete a guide or workflow and need supporting materials — a template to fill out, a tool to install, or a policy document to review — check the Resources page first. It's organized by topic so you can quickly find what's relevant to the work you're doing.
</Accordion>

</Note>
