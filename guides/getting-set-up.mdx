Starting a new role can feel overwhelming, but a clear setup routine makes all the difference. This guide walks you through every essential step for your first day — from logging into your accounts and understanding your responsibilities to finding key documents and booking your first check-in with your manager. By the end, you will have everything you need to work productively from day one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Access Your Accounts and Tools">
    Before you can do anything else, make sure you have access to every platform your role requires. Check your onboarding email for login credentials and activation links. Sign in to your company email, project management tool, internal chat platform (such as Slack or Teams), and any role-specific software listed in your offer letter or onboarding pack. If any account is missing or a password reset fails, contact your IT helpdesk immediately so the issue is resolved before your first full working day.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Your Role and Responsibilities">
    Locate your job description, role charter, or onboarding document and read it carefully. Pay attention to your core responsibilities, the goals set for your first 30, 60, and 90 days, and the key performance indicators (KPIs) your manager will use to evaluate your work. Highlight anything that is unclear so you can address it in your first check-in. Understanding expectations early prevents misalignment and sets you up for measurable success.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Meet Your Team">
    Introduce yourself to your immediate teammates through your company's chat platform or in person if you share a physical office. Send a short, friendly message to your direct colleagues and any cross-functional partners listed in your onboarding materials. Ask your manager for an org chart or a list of key contacts across departments. Building relationships early creates a support network you can rely on when questions and challenges arise later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Up Your Workspace">
    A well-organized workspace — both physical and digital — directly supports your focus and productivity. Arrange your desk or home office so your monitor is at eye level, your keyboard and mouse are within comfortable reach, and cables are tidy. On the digital side, pin your most-used apps to your taskbar or dock, configure your email signature with your full name and role, set your working-hours status in your calendar, and create a folder structure in your cloud storage that mirrors how your team organizes shared files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Key Documents and Policies">
    Navigate to your company's intranet or HR portal and locate the employee handbook, code of conduct, data privacy policy, and any department-specific operating procedures. Read each document and note the sections that apply directly to your day-to-day work. Understanding policies around communication, data handling, time-off requests, and expense reporting prevents costly mistakes and ensures you stay compliant from the start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Schedule Your First Check-In">
    Before the end of day one, book a 30-minute meeting with your manager for later in the week. Use this session to align on your immediate priorities, ask the questions you have accumulated, and confirm how and when you will receive regular feedback. Recurring one-on-ones are one of the most valuable tools for staying on track, so agree on a cadence — weekly or bi-weekly — and add it to both calendars right away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Ask questions as soon as they come up — do not save them for a more convenient moment. Your first weeks on the job are the one time when curiosity is universally expected. Colleagues and managers would far rather answer a quick question now than troubleshoot a misunderstanding later. Keep a running list in a notes app so you can batch smaller questions when you have your manager's time.
</Tip>

<Note>
  All HR forms, benefits enrollment links, payroll setup instructions, and official onboarding checklists live in your company HR portal. If you were not given a direct link during hiring, ask your HR contact or People Operations team. You can also find curated onboarding resources linked from the Job Knowledge Base under [Resources](/reference/resources).
</Note>
